# Bauhaus: background and sources

Walter Gropius founded the Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar in 1919 by merging the school of arts and crafts with the academy of fine arts. It moved to Dessau in 1925 (Gropius's asymmetric pinwheel-plan building of concrete and glass), and to Berlin in 1932, closing in 1933 under Nazi pressure. Its aim: unite art, craft and industry; function over ornament; the "Gesamtkunstwerk" (total work of art). Its teachers spread modernism worldwide after emigrating.

| Teacher | What to borrow for motion |
|---|---|
| Johannes Itten | Preliminary course: materials, composition, colour contrasts, warm vs cool, colour and personality. |
| Wassily Kandinsky | Colour as music; yellow = triangle, red = square, blue = circle; point, line and plane. Visual music, shapes reacting to sound. |
| Paul Klee | Line as "a walk"; rhythm, playful geometry. |
| Josef Albers | *Interaction of Color*: colours change with neighbours; *Homage to the Square* nested squares. |
| László Moholy-Nagy | "Typophoto" (type + photography), photomontage, light and motion (Light-Space Modulator), New Vision. |
| Oskar Schlemmer | *Triadic Ballet* (premiere 1922): dancers as geometric figures; yellow, pink and black acts; body built from cylinders, circles, cones. |
| Herbert Bayer | *Universal* alphabet (1925): single lowercase alphabet from circle and horizontal bar, no serifs; asymmetric layouts; red and black with grotesk/Futura; later corporate design in the US. |
| Joost Schmidt, Marianne Brandt | Advertising and typographic design; metal product design. |

Modern "Bauhaus pattern" (tiles of quarter circles, semicircles and squares in primaries) is a contemporary homage, not historical Bauhaus work; it animates very well.
