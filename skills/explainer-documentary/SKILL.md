# Explainer documentary: format, story, scene plan

Distilled from 4 Aevy TV documentaries (Brainrot, Dumb Design of Indian Cities, Psychedelics, their
company story) and a Claude + Higgsfield Vox tutorial. Aevy TV is the target: a cinematic,
presenter-led Indian explainer that uses Vox-style graphics as inserts, not as the whole video.

Run in this order and get the user's approval at each step: 1 topic and angle, 2 outline, 3 script,
4 scene plan, then hand off to design and animation.

## 1. The format (the "shell")

Every episode cycles through four modes:

1. **Presenter on a themed set.** One host talks to camera from a set designed for the topic, styled
   like a magazine shoot (white studio with an armchair, a filing cabinet growing plants and a CRT TV
   for attention; a garden with a waterfall for psychedelics; a neon red/blue city-construction
   backdrop for urban planning; a warm wooden desk with blind shadows for money).
2. **Cinematic B-roll and staged scenes.** Low-key light, one practical lamp, close-ups of hands,
   faces and objects; conspiracy boards with sticky notes and red string; REC viewfinder overlays.
3. **Graphic explainers.** Vox-style inserts (maps, charts, diagrams, collages) for every mechanism,
   number and place. Each episode gets its own palette and texture (see vox-collage-design).
4. **Recognisable media.** TikToks, game shows, app screens on a phone mockup, finance charts,
   news headlines, archive footage.

Consistent shell across episodes: small channel watermark top-right, small footage credit line
bottom-left ("Footage: YouTube/Deccan Herald"), a thin yellow chapter progress bar along the bottom,
clean sans + elegant serif typography, lots of negative space.

## 2. Story structure

- **Cold open, one of:**
  - Personal observation in first person, a list of small, specific details, then the threat named
    in one line. ("Lately, I've been noticing things... cab drivers speeding through reels... mothers on
    level 6000 of Candy Crush." / "Something is rotting our attention.")
  - Visual comparison: "Look at these two images." Reveal what's hidden ("once you see it, you cannot
    unsee it").
  - Comedic sketch or argument between the team that frames the debate, self-aware and meta.
- **Set the stakes and the roadmap:** a big contrast ("We landed on the moon, but our cities have no
  footpaths"), then "That's the question we'll be investigating in the next 25 minutes."
- **Chapters by named concept** (a brain region, a rule, a system), each with its own graphic world.
  Signpost: "So let's break it down." / "That's our first pit stop."
- **Every claim = mechanism + number + named source.** Show HOW it works (CoComelon's two-screen test;
  the notification delay that creates anticipation), then a hard stat with its source on screen
  (UNODC, a university study, a named book and author).
- **Escalate from them to you:** "Clearly terrifying for children. But what about you?" Then put the
  viewer in a scenario: "Imagine you're just back home from work..."
- **One metaphor carried through** ("dopamine cartel", "virus", "symptoms").
- **Act out the topic** for humour: the host "forgets" mid-sentence in the attention episode.
- **Sponsor as a skit transition** (the distracted host greets the sponsor).
- **Local first:** rupees, Indian cities and families, with global comparisons for contrast.
- End on a question or a call to change, not a summary.

## 3. Voice and writing

- Conversational, confident, a little sardonic. Short sentences. Rhetorical questions.
- Reveal beats: "Today? Forty-seven seconds."
- Second person ("you") for stakes; first person ("I started noticing") for curiosity.
- Short-form Vox variant (~60 s): stat hook (then vs now) -> it's everywhere (two named places) ->
  graph with a turning-point year -> meet one person -> their day in percentages -> closing question.
- Narration: the host's own voice for presenter parts. For VO-only pieces use a warm, calm documentary
  narrator in Fish Audio (connector) at medium pace.

## 4. Scene plan (the "editor brain")

Produce a table, one row per line that deserves a visual:

| # | Script line | Mode (set / B-roll / graphic / media) | What the viewer should feel or notice | Visual and animation | Source/credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:
- **Perform the sentence:** the visual changes on the exact word that carries the meaning ("falls off a
  cliff" -> the line drops on "cliff").
- **One new element per beat;** elements stack up, never all at once.
- Numbers become physical (counter card, clock, stacks, a pie that fills). Places become maps (city ->
  world, red ink circles, travel-time routes). Trends become hand-drawn charts with the key year circled,
  then a zoom-in on it. People become one recurring cutout character with a label ("THE AVERAGE
  WORKER"). Abstract ideas get a literal metaphor (apps "pulling her hand back" -> a hand reaching in).
- Never still: every shot has a slow push or drift.
- Each section: 2-3 designs that transition into one another.
- Alternate modes so no mode runs longer than ~20-30 s.

Then hand off: look -> vox-collage-design; motion -> vox-after-effects-recipes; live-action and
AI B-roll shots -> cinematography-for-ai; assembly -> premiere-pro connector (VO on A1, scenes on V1,
music bed on A2 ducked under voice, paper/pen SFX on A3).

## 5. Short-form explainer and tutorial reels

For 35-95 s vertical pieces, the study of 30 reels (reel-craft/references) gives a tighter shell than the long-form one above.

- **Talking head plus screen recording.** Open with an illustrated intro world (12 s in the one reel that did this; still start motion by 0.4 s and land the hook line by about 2 s), then the speaker in a locked medium close-up, then the screen recording in the top two thirds with the speaker in the bottom third. Middle steps can be screen and illustrated plates only, changing every 1.5-3 s. Return the speaker for the call to action. Keep the face clear of captions (formats-and-recipes.md, format 5).
- **Voice-driven caption clock.** Captions run 1-3 words, 0.2-0.35 s per word, one emphasis device on one word per beat (italic serif swap or one accent colour). Cut on the first syllable of the next phrase, not the breath; put big moves in the 0.3-0.5 s gaps (kinetic-type-and-captions.md, sections 2, 4, 8).
- **Metaphor per sentence.** One object or picture per sentence, 2-4 s each, the old one leaving as the new one arrives. Hold one full-screen word card for the pivot at about half runtime (pacing-and-timing.md; formats-and-recipes.md, format 11).
- **Chat-story device.** Message bubbles as scenes, with colour carrying meaning: green for sent or success, red for loss, magenta for emphasis. Escalate numbers in three steps, each with a glow flash (visual-design.md, section 3; kinetic-type-and-captions.md, section 6).
- **Evidence board.** Keep it to the sticky-note and red-string set piece from section 1; the reel study gave no separate timing for it.
- **Comment-keyword call to action.** Show the offer before asking. Build the keyword in accent italic serif in quotes over about 2 s while the presenter points at the comment field, then hold 2 s (formats-and-recipes.md, format 10).
