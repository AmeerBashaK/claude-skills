# Neo-brutalism: background and sources

Brutalism comes from architecture: the French "béton brut" (raw concrete), 1950s buildings that put function over form and exposed their structure. On the web, "brutalist" design (around 2014 onward, late 2010s in wider use) reacted against polished, uniform sites with bare HTML: system fonts, blue links, monospace, visible seams, often confrontational and sometimes hard to use (Craigslist and the Drudge Report are cited as accidental ancestors). Neo-brutalism (neubrutalism, early 2020s) is the friendlier, systematic version popularised by product and startup sites (Gumroad's 2021 redesign is often cited; verify any live example): thick borders, hard 0-blur offset shadows, flat saturated clashing colour, supersized quirky type with plain body text, and visible structure, while keeping clear hierarchy and working affordances.

Accessibility: black text on bright fills usually gives very high contrast (vendor checks report e.g. 7.9:1 on hot pink, 21:1 on white), and thick borders aid hierarchy. Cautions: large pure-black + saturated areas can cause eye strain; check every pair with a WCAG checker; provide visible focus states in live UI.
