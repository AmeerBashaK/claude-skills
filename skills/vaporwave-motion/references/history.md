# Vaporwave: background and sources

Vaporwave emerged online (Tumblr, Reddit, Bandcamp) around 2010–2012. Oneohtrix Point Never's *Chuck Person's Eccojams Vol. 1* (2010) is often called the first; Macintosh Plus's *Floral Shoppe* (2011, Vektroid; Helios bust on a pink checker grid) is its most famous release. The name riffs on "vaporware" (announced but never released products). The music slows and loops corporate and consumer audio (muzak, smooth jazz, infomercials); the visuals collage 80s/90s consumer tech, Japanese text, classical statues, malls and early-web graphics, influenced by the Memphis Group's postmodern design and seapunk. The visual style outlived and outgrew the music.

Its stance is deliberately ambiguous: critique of consumer capitalism, ironic celebration, or plain nostalgia. Some artists (e.g. Vektroid) have said it isn't meant as anti-capitalist; much of the political reading comes from critics and academics. Japanese text reflects the 80s/90s Western fascination with Japan as a tech superpower. Sub-genres: mallsoft (e.g. *Palm Mall*), future funk, hardvapour, and adjacent seapunk.

**vs synthwave:** both are 80s-nostalgic and emerged around 2010 with neon and grids, but synthwave is a sincere, glossy celebration of 80s film/game culture, while vaporwave is slowed, lo-fi, pastel, collage-based and ironic about consumer culture.
