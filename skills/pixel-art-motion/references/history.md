# Pixel art: background and sources

Pixel art grew from the hardware limits of 1970s–90s computers and consoles (Atari, NES, Game Boy, SNES, Amiga, arcade): low resolutions and small palettes forced every pixel and colour to count. Techniques that emerged: hand anti-aliasing, dithering, colour ramps with hue shifting, sub-pixel animation, palette (colour) cycling (Mark Ferrari's animated scenes for LucasArts and *Living Worlds*), and sprite animation with few frames. Since the 2000s it's a deliberate aesthetic in indie games (Celeste, Shovel Knight, Hollow Knight is hand-drawn but often cited for frame counts, Dead Cells uses 3D-to-pixel), art, UI and motion design. Communities: Pixel Joint, Lospec (palettes). Key book: Michael Azzi's *Pixel Logic*.

Shipped frame counts often cited: Celeste run 4 frames, Shovel Knight walk 6 frames; walk = contact, passing, contact, passing with a 1-px bob at 16×16.
