# Risograph: background and sources

The Risograph is a digital stencil duplicator made by Riso Kagaku (Japan, introduced 1986), designed for fast, cheap high-volume copying in schools and offices. Artists, zine makers and indie publishers adopted it in the 2000s–2010s for its bright spot inks (including fluorescents), low cost and handmade feel. It prints one soy-based ink per pass from a drum; multi-colour work needs one pass per ink, so registration drifts. Ink sits on the paper surface and dries matte with grain; inks are translucent, so the paper colour and overlaps change the result. There are roughly 20–35 standard ink colours depending on the source and machine; screen colours never match exactly, and fluorescent pink cannot be reproduced on screen.

Production notes from print shops: trapping reduces visible misregistration; large solids can jam the machine or show roller marks, so break them up or keep density ≤ 90%; add bleed and safe zones because alignment shifts.
