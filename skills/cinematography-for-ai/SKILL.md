# Cinematography for AI and motion

From StudioBinder Shot List Ep 6 (camera movement), Ep 7 (lenses), Ep 11 (composition) and Dan Kieft's
"All camera movement prompts for AI filmmaking" (Higgsfield, Seedance + Kling).

## Camera moves: intent and when to use
**Tripod**
- Static (ECU/CU/MS/WS): locked off; forces focus on the subject.
- Pan / whip pan: scan, follow, reveal off-screen; whip = fast blurred transition.
- Tilt up/down: show height; looking up at a giant makes the subject small/weak; feet-to-face.
- Slow zoom in: tension, focus on what's said. Slow zoom out: detach, subject becomes insignificant.
- Fast zoom: discovery, something's wrong. Crash zoom in: shock, realisation, comic emphasis.
  Crash zoom out: reveal an absurd context (the suit with no trousers).
- Dolly in/out: camera moves, lens fixed (real perspective change). Dolly in: creeping, jump-scare
  setup. Dolly out: defeated, isolated, "leaving them behind". Zoom = flat; dolly = parallax.
- Dolly zoom (vertigo): dolly in + zoom out, background warps; unease or realisation.

**On a track**
- Truck (sideways, parallel), pedestal (straight up/down; feet-to-head character intro), slider (short
  slide across a desk of evidence), push-past (foreground obstacle passes the lens into a hidden moment),
  arc (behind to face), orbit (360, action/fantasy).

**Following the subject**
- Tracking through a busy space; over-the-shoulder follow (see what they see; suspense); reverse
  tracking (walk backwards in front, lock on emotion); side tracking (running); low tracking (boots,
  mystery); vehicle tracking; chase (messy, scrambling).

**Human**
- Handheld: real, raw, uncomfortable (tense arguments). Body-mounted/snorricam: head frozen, world
  shakes (panic, drunk). Use human moves to stop AI shots feeling robotic.

**Big moves and special**
- Crane up (lift away at a scene's end, reveal the city around one person), drone flyover/push-in/
  pullback/FPV through windows, helicopter (steady grand establishing).
- First-person POV, tilt-shift (miniature world), infinite zoom (surreal), Earth zoom-out
  (person -> city -> planet), time-lapse.

## Composition
- Rule of thirds and power points; two balanced focal points.
- Leading lines pull the eye to the subject.
- Negative space; headroom; lead/nose room in the direction they face. Too much space = isolation
  (use on purpose).
- Depth: foreground, middle ground, background; deep focus vs shallow focus.
- Symmetry = order/power; asymmetric balance = tension.

## Lenses
- Wide (<35mm, 17-24mm): shows more, exaggerates distance, can distort; establishing shots, sets.
- Standard (35-50mm): close to the eye; natural presenter shots.
- Telephoto (70mm+, 85/135mm): narrow, compresses space, isolates the subject with shallow depth of field;
  intimate close-ups.
- Fisheye: strong curve; comedy or disorientation. Macro: 1:1 extreme detail (clues, textures).
- Prime: wider aperture, shallower DOF. Anamorphic: wide frame, oval bokeh, horizontal flares.

## Prompt template (AI video)
`[shot size] of [subject + action], [camera move] [speed], [lens mm], [depth of field], [lighting], [mood]`
- "medium close-up of the presenter in a white armchair, slow dolly in, 85mm, shallow depth of field,
  single practical lamp, low-key, tense"
- "extreme wide establishing shot of Mumbai at dusk, slow drone push-in, 24mm, deep focus, haze"
- "close-up of a hand reaching for a phone on a dark desk, slider move left to right, 50mm, handheld feel"

## Doc defaults (Aevy/Vox)
- Presenter: MCU/CU, 50-85mm, slow push-in on key lines, crash zoom on punchlines, cut to a second angle
  for emphasis, handheld for raw moments.
- B-roll: low-key, practical light, macro details, slider and push-past moves.
- Graphics (After Effects/Blender): treat the virtual camera like a dolly/truck/arc through 3D cutout
  layers; 35mm camera; Earth zoom-out for maps; push past foreground collage pieces for depth.
