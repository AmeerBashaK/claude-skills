# Y2K chrome: background and sources

Y2K Futurism grew from the approach of the year 2000: tech optimism mixed with millennium-bug anxiety, and the dot-com boom pushing brands to look futuristic. Its visual shorthand: reflective chrome and liquid-silver gradients, mirror-finish text, iridescent and holographic colour (pinks, purples, blues, greens), bubbly inflated 3D forms, translucent plastic, lens flares, glossy icons and techno type. The term "Y2K aesthetic" is credited to Evan Collins of the Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute (CARI); "Y2K Futurism" separates the style from early-2000s culture in general. It has been revived since the late 2010s (fashion, music, social), with spin-offs like cybersigilism and the Frutiger Aero nostalgia wave.

| Reference | What to borrow |
|---|---|
| Late-90s/2000s music videos and album art (Hype Williams era, Daft Punk, Britney "Toxic") | Chrome, glossy futurism, flares. |
| iMac G3, translucent electronics | Candy-coloured see-through plastic. |
| Designers Republic, Tomato, early Flash sites | Techno type, UI-like layouts, chrome and grids. |
| Frutiger Aero (Windows Vista/7 era, 2004–2013) | Glossy aqua, bubbles, nature + tech. |
| 2020s revival: chrome type on Instagram/TikTok, cybersigilism | Liquid chrome lettering, tribal chrome, holographic pop. |

Modern guidance: use a few signals on key elements (hero, CTA), not everywhere; avoid it where trust is the primary job.
