# 08 — Study Notes: "Give Me 7 Minutes & Your Web Design Skills Will Take Off"

Source: Self-Made Web Designer (Chris Misterek), YouTube `1NTKwpAVcHg`, 7:14.
Files: `Training Data/Principles study/f-7min-web-design/` (video.mp4, transcript.txt, video.en.vtt, frames/sheet_01–10.jpg).

**Thesis:** almost all web design knowledge comes down to **3 pillars: layout, colour, typography**, and none of it matters without **conversion** and the **right audience**.

## 1. Layout: use visual hierarchy, not the F-pattern
- The "people read in an F" rule is outdated. Forcing content into an F can make people miss things.
- NN/g (shown on screen) lists several scanning patterns: **F-pattern, layer-cake** (scanning headings), **spotted** (hunting for a word or link), **marking** (eyes fixed while scrolling, common on mobile), **bypassing** (skipping repeated first words), **commitment** (reading everything, rare).
- So design for **hierarchy**: the most important element in each section is the biggest and boldest, and everything else gets "turned down in volume". Demo: an example site with the hero headline "Making Sustainability Happen" boxed as read 1st → 2nd → 3rd.
- **Buttons/CTAs need high contrast** with everything around them. The main thing we want visitors to do is click.
- **Don't use ghost buttons** (outline only, no fill) for primary actions. People don't see them and don't click. (Apple's solid blue "Buy" pill was shown as the good example.)

## 2. Colour
- The palette must **match the client's brand**.
- **Accessibility first:** contrast high enough for visually impaired users. Tool: **coolors.co → Contrast Checker**. Paste the brand colours and it shows which pairs pass.
- **60-30-10 rule:** 60% dominant neutrals (greys, black, white), 30% brand colours, 10% accent for CTAs and buttons. It's a starting guideline, not a law. (Example site: neutral background, soft-blue service tags, the 10% being a neon-yellow "Start with a call" button.)

## 3. Typography
- Underrated, but it drives readability and UX. Families: serif, sans, handwritten, script, display, monospace.
- Three core text roles:
  - **H1**: the biggest, most prominent text, describing what the whole page is about. One per page.
  - **H2**: subheadings that divide the page and guide attention.
  - **P (body)**: never decorative or script fonts. They look cool but kill readability.

## 4. Conversion beats pretty
- Story: a redesign that was "clean, modern, chef's kiss" made **sales drop** after launch, because it optimised for looks over conversion.
- Conversion = **reducing friction**. Make it stupidly easy to find things and buy or sign up. Three levers:
  1. **Clarity**: who this is, what the visitor should do next, why they should care.
  2. **Scannability**: people scan, they don't read. Lay content out so they can jump around and decide.
  3. **Motivation**: speak to what's driving their yes or no, not just to their eyes.

## 5. Design for the audience
Not for yourself, not for the client. **Research who the visitors are** and choose fonts, colours and layout for them.

## 6. Keep learning
The industry changes fast, and what worked five years ago may not work now. AI won't take all design jobs, but it will take lazy designers' jobs.

## Methods to reuse
| Method | How |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy check | Number elements in each section 1 → 2 → 3 by importance, then check size, weight and contrast follow that order |
| CTA check | Solid fill, accent colour (the 10%), highest contrast in its section, never a ghost button for the primary action |
| Contrast check | coolors.co contrast checker, or WebAIM. AA = 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text |
| Palette build | Neutrals 60 / brand 30 / accent 10 |
| Type roles | One H1 per page, H2 per section, plain readable P |
| Conversion pass | Ask "is it clear? scannable? motivating?" for every section |

- New: **drop the F-pattern as a rule**, **ban ghost primary buttons**, and the **clarity / scannability / motivation** conversion test (added to 06).
