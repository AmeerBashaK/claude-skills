# 07 — Study Notes: The Futur Web Design Reviews

Distilled from the two videos in `Training Data/Principles study/` (transcripts + frames there).

## A. "Web design review": Blind studio site redesign
1. **Sitemap first.** Home → Work → Feed → Events → About → Contact, sketched before any layout.
2. **4-column grid** on desktop that collapses to 2 or 1 on mobile. Design the mobile strip and the desktop frame together.
3. **The hero is everything.** A full-bleed striking image, but the **headline matters more**. If visitors don't see or hear what they want above the fold, they never scroll.
4. Web vocabulary comes from newspapers: masthead, above the fold, byline, pull quote.
5. **Featured work** = a curated, numbered order (it needs a CMS "featured" tag and a sort index), plus a "View all projects" link.
6. **Multiple paths** to the same content. Don't make people scroll back up.
7. **Logo quilt** = validation. Clients want proof you've worked with companies of their size and vertical. Real clients only.
8. **Hover overlays** (dim the image, show the info) keep grids clean. Plan a mobile alternative since touch has no hover.
9. **Case study** = Client → Challenge → Solution/Outcome, then text chunks paired with images. Break up one-column monotony with headline, subhead, callouts, bullets and body. Editorial rhythm.
10. Two templates: custom case study vs. standard project (image flow). Systematise to save build time.
11. Avoid heavy type-over-image overlaps. They're hard to build and look good only in comps.
12. **Copy hook:** a provocative line ("We build bridges"), then explain it in pairs (strategy conceived ↔ executed, brands ↔ customers, goals ↔ results). Sell the approach; the work is the byproduct.
13. End with a **clear CTA** and a newsletter (a low-frequency value exchange).

## B. "Website from wireframes": Oli's fishing lodge
1. **Wireframes = boxes + placeholder copy** showing architecture, features and functions. Built from the discovery session.
2. The first design pass usually just paints the wireframe ("too digital, template-y"). Expect to iterate. **Chip away at the marble**: block first, refine (sand) later.
3. **Brand keywords drive every decision**: "refined rustic". Check each comp against them. Corporate-clean failed the brief.
4. **Details tell the truth**: photo border proportions (photo vs. Polaroid), the right trees, the right knife. Immerse yourself in the subject's culture.
5. **Depth, not flatness.** Add a light source, vignette and shadows. Bend shadows so paper and photos curl instead of lying sandwiched between glass. Layer 3–4 paper pieces for depth without using more space.
6. **Never blur everything.** Use selective focus: keep a plane sharp and blur the rest, like shallow depth of field.
7. **Break boxiness**: blend landscape photo edges into the page, add torn edges, tape, coffee rings, photocopy textures. Perfectly imperfect.
8. **Type fit**: the script italic fought the ribbon path, so use a simpler sans there. Distressed type for patina, but keep it legible.
9. **Fill trapped negative space** with decorative motifs (stars, rules); that's why vintage badges have them.
10. Dutch angle + selective focus + vignette + colour grade turned a flat snapshot into "you were there".
11. **Closure** (Scott McCloud): two images side by side imply a story (a man fishing + a fish caught).
12. Vignette to pull the eye inward. Reduce contrast or saturation of elements that pull the eye away.
13. Five or six Photoshop tricks (levels + masks for light shafts, shadows, selective blur, vignette, grade) used in combination do most of the work.
14. Process: give designers time and room to explore multiple options, sketch fixes on paper (30 seconds versus hours in Illustrator), and treat it as collaborative.
15. Giant atmospheric images will worry developers, so plan optimisation (see 05).

## How this maps to 3D web
The same lessons apply in WebGL: light the scene (HDRI + key + rim), add depth of field and vignette in post, avoid flat even lighting, layer foreground, mid and background for parallax, and keep the details true to the brand world.
