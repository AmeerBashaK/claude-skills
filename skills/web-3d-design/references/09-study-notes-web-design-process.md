# 09 — Study Notes: "Complete Web Design Process: What Took Me 10 Years to Learn in 12 Minutes"

Source: Self-Made Web Designer (Chris Misterek), YouTube `T5JglDcd54A`, 12:13.
Files: `Training Data/Principles study/f-web-design-process/` (transcript.txt, video.en.vtt; the video download was blocked by YouTube, but the notes come from the transcript).
This one is about **project process and client management**, not visual design.

## The 4 phases
**Onboarding → Design & Development → Launch → Offboarding**

### 1. Onboarding
1. **Discovery call**: check the fit. Make the **client the star**. Ask about their business, hopes and dreams, frustrations with the current site, and frustrations with past freelancers. Listen more than you pitch (it's like a date, not a monologue).
2. **Proposal → contract → first payment.** No signed contract and first payment means no project, "just a lovely idea".
3. **Over-communicate.** Replying to emails **within 48h beats about 90% of competitors**.
4. **Kickoff call**: set timeline and deadlines **for both sides** (copy, images, brand assets, feedback). Waiting on client assets is the #1 thing that derails projects.
5. **Deadlines with consequences**: a fee, or the feedback window closes. **24h to respond to revisions.** People use all the time you give them.

### 2. Design & Development: add complexity in small steps
Never start straight on the homepage. The client can't say what they want until they see something, and you'll redo work.
1. **Sitemap**: every page and how they connect.
2. **Wireframe, "low lo-fi"**: a **content outline only** ("section 1 is about X, section 2 about Y"). **No design decisions at all**, not even fonts or boxes, so the client never gets attached to placeholder choices. (Story: client approved a boilerplate wireframe font, then wanted it back over the real design.) Tool: Octopus.do (has AI sitemap generation).
3. **Homepage design**: takes the most time. It sets the design language, it's usually the longest page, and its sections get reused on other pages.
4. **Present with reasoning**, never just a URL. Send a **Loom walkthrough** or do a call explaining *why* each element is there. Clients don't know what good design is, so give them the reasons. This prevents most revisions.
5. **Remaining pages** are built from homepage sections, then reviewed.

**Revision policy:** **one revision round per stage** (sitemap+wireframe / homepage / other pages), each with a 24h feedback window. Once a round closes it stays closed. Reopening costs extra or is a goodwill gesture. The designer is also the **project manager**: keeping things moving helps the client.

### 3. Launch
- Pick a **low-traffic launch date** when platform support is available.
- **Use it yourself and test everything**: submit every form, test-buy products, sign up for lead magnets, confirm the emails arrive. Never launch and then have to pull it back.

### 4. Offboarding (doubles or triples lifetime client revenue)
- **Grace period** of support in the first weeks (how to log in, add a post, add a product).
- **Client help docs**: an FAQ document plus **site-specific Loom tutorials** they keep, like SaaS help docs.
- **Keep following up** at a comfortable pace: launch anniversary, birthdays, business anniversaries. "How's business? How's the site?" Repeat clients skip discovery, and some stay for a decade.

## Methods to reuse
| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| Discovery questions | Business goals · dreams · current site frustrations · past freelancer frustrations · audience |
| Two-sided timeline | Your milestones + client asset/feedback deadlines, in the contract |
| 24h feedback rule | Each revision window = 24h, then the round closes |
| 1 revision per stage | Sitemap/wireframe → homepage → inner pages |
| Content-only wireframe | Section-by-section purpose list, zero visual decisions |
| Homepage-first system | Design the homepage fully, reuse its sections as the component library |
| Reasoned presentation | Loom or call: "this is here because…" |
| Pre-launch use-it-yourself test | Forms, checkout, lead magnet, emails, mobile |
| Offboarding kit | FAQ doc + Loom tutorials + follow-up calendar |

## Compared with the rest of the library
- **Wireframe disagreement:** The Futur (07) uses box wireframes with placeholder copy. This video argues for **content-only outlines** so clients don't anchor on placeholder visuals. Pick based on the client: box wireframes help when layout is the question, content outlines when the client tends to fixate on visuals. Either way, label wireframes clearly as "structure only, no design".
- Agrees with 02: sitemap → wireframe → design, systematise templates (homepage sections = the reusable system).
- For our own motion/3D web work: the "present with reasoning" Loom works well as a screen-recorded walkthrough of the site's motion and scroll moments.
