Part 1 of a layout series: **focal point, white space, hierarchy.**

## The three basics
**1. Focal point**: the *centre of interest*, not the centre of the layout.
- Solid beats outlined: a filled circle wins over stroked ones because it has more presence.
- Where lines meet is a focal point.
- **Rule of thirds**: put the subject's point of weight (a bird's head or neck) on a third line or intersection instead of dead centre. It's more interesting and shows more context. Works for layouts as well as photos.

**2. White space**: *quiet areas of visual rest* that give other elements room to breathe.
- **It doesn't have to be white.** A blurred photo background, sea and sky, or an empty grid cell all count.
- The music analogy: an orchestra's loud moment only hits because of the quiet passages. Layouts need dynamics too.
- Not every grid cell needs filling. Empty cells feel more premium and relaxed.
- Full-bleed photos can still have white space: compose so the quiet areas (sky, water) hold the logo, nav, headline and CTA.

**3. Hierarchy**: give priority to what matters most.
- "If everything's on 10, nothing is emphasised." It's like mixing a track: lead vocal forward, the rest behind.
- **Orient, then lead**: like a "you are here" map, first tell users where they are, then take them on a journey.

## Good vs. bad examples
| Site | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Camera hero (demo) | ✅ | Camera = focal point, white space around image and copy, headline clearly above body |
| Cliff photo hero | ✅ | Full-bleed photo with quiet sea/sky zones holding nav, headline, CTA |
| Magazine spread | ✅ | Spread-spanning photo pulls you in, headline > body, empty grid cells |
| **Figma** home | ✅ | Big bold headline, generous white space, the USP (collaboration) is clear before scrolling |
| **Webflow** home | ✅ | Same structure inverted (white on dark), small logo, minimal nav (login/register), value prop first |
| **Pantone** home | ❌ | No clear focal point (centre type vs. biggest headline vs. right image), **rotating carousel** distracts more, heavy solid blocks, all-caps coloured type (harder to read), elements crowding each other. A brand for designers that fails the basics |

## Methods to reuse
- **Stop pushing pixels around.** Take your hand off the mouse and diagnose the layout against focal point, white space and hierarchy.
- Annotate references: circle the focal point, shade the white space, number the hierarchy 1-2-3. Train your eye on real sites.
- Avoid hero carousels. Pick one focal message.

## How it connects
Confirms 01, 08 and 10: one dominant element, white space as dynamics, hierarchy over everything. The "white space doesn't have to be white" idea matters for 3D web: a soft-focus or fogged 3D background is white space, so the foreground object and headline become the focal point.
