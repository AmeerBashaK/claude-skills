# Flat 2D: background, studios and sources

Flat design is a reaction against photorealism and skeuomorphism: simple geometric shapes, solid colours, and little or no depth (no bevels, textures, lighting effects). In motion it became the default explainer look in the 2010s because it is fast to produce and render, scales cleanly, and reads instantly. Its roots go back to mid-century modern illustration and UPA's limited animation (1950s), Saul Bass titles and Swiss poster flatness.

| Reference | What to borrow |
|---|---|
| UPA (Gerald McBoing-Boing), Saul Bass titles | Mid-century flat: limited palettes, angular shapes, graphic staging, limited animation. |
| Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell | Semi-flat science style: dark backgrounds, saturated colour, rounded forms, glows and gradients, huge zooms through scale. (Details are observations from their videos, not their own documented method.) |
| IBM Design Language (flat illustration) | Strict flat rules: geometric construction, opaque fills, minimum 8 px shapes; animate with position/rotation/scale, avoid exaggerated bounces, long fades and canvas-wide camera moves. |
| Google / Material illustration | Clean geometric construction, consistent corner radii, bold colour. |
| Corporate explainer studios (Ordinary Folk, Buck, Giant Ant, Oddfellows) | Character acting, overlap and appeal with simple flat forms; polished easing. |
| Duik (RxLaboratory) | The free After Effects rigging tool: structures, auto-rig, IK, walk cycles, Kleaner for overlap. |
